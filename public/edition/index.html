The lead

The weapon may be local; the targeting system is not.

The investigation matters because intelligence support can change battlefield accuracy without a foreign military firing the missile itself.
01 · Gulf security, intelligence & energyAssociated Press investigation · Tuesday

Russian spy satellites repeatedly converged over Saudi targets before 40 missile and drone attacks reviewed by a private intelligence firm.

What changed

AP reports that Russian government and military satellites gathered over Saudi oil and military sites before attacks by Iranian-backed Houthis and Iraqi militias; officials and a Houthi leader said Russian-derived intelligence reached the groups through Iranian advisers.

Why it matters

This would turn the Gulf conflict from a regional proxy war into a wider intelligence contest: modest weapons become more dangerous when a major power supplies location, timing and post-strike assessment.

Watch next

Whether governments release corroborating imagery, whether Riyadh attributes specific strikes to Russian support, and what countermeasures follow. The orbital correlation and interviews are reported; AP could not independently prove who tasked each satellite or tied every pass to a particular strike.

A repeated orbital pattern is strong evidence of support—but not a public chain of command

AP's reporting combines satellite analysis with interviews rather than relying on one anonymous allegation. Geneva-based AURORAH Geosignal Intelligence reviewed 40 missile and drone attacks against Saudi Arabia between 1 July and 19 October and said Russian government or military satellites passed over the target areas for days before and after every strike. AP also spoke with three officials, including a senior Houthi figure, and a private analyst who said Iranian advisers converted Russian-derived intelligence into target lists or operational guidance. The article connects that support to attacks on the East–West oil pipeline and a military air base where an Italian fighter was damaged. The limit is equally important: a satellite's presence does not reveal its orders, and neither the raw tasking records nor the intelligence products are public. The investigation establishes a pattern and multiple-source attribution, not a declassified command log.

You already know

The reviewed orbital activity, the strikes and the officials' accounts are documented by AP; Saudi officials have publicly alleged outside intelligence support.

New now

The investigation supplies a systematic 40-strike pattern and describes how Iranian advisers allegedly moved Russian-derived data to armed groups.

Observed pattern

Russian state or military satellites appeared over all 40 reviewed target areas around the time of attack, according to AURORAH.

Evidence limit

Orbital coincidence does not by itself disclose tasking, product delivery or causation for each strike.

World beyond the lead

The electorate moved, but the governing majority did not appear.

The next contest is not the count alone; it is whether parties that reject partnership can still coordinate enough to seat a government.
02 · Israel, coalition politics & regional policyAssociated Press · overnight

Israel's exit polls put the opposition narrowly ahead, but neither bloc has a path to the 61 seats needed to govern.

What changed

The first national vote since the 7 October 2023 attack appears to have reduced Benjamin Netanyahu's bloc to about 50 seats, versus 52–53 for Gadi Eisenkot's opposition, with Arab parties holding an estimated 17–18.

Why it matters

The result is a rebuke without a transfer of power. Coalition bargaining could leave Netanyahu as caretaker for weeks or produce another election while decisions on Gaza, Iran and the West Bank remain live.

Watch next

Official results due later this week, President Isaac Herzog's consultations, and whether Arab-led parties support a minority government from outside it. These are exit-poll estimates, not a declared result, and small changes can alter coalition arithmetic.

Israel chooses parties first and a prime minister through coalition arithmetic afterward

No single Israeli party is expected to approach a majority in the 120-seat Knesset. Exit polls reported by AP placed Netanyahu's right-wing bloc on about 50 seats, Eisenkot's opposition on 52 or 53, and Arab-led parties on 17 or 18. That gives neither principal bloc the 61 votes required to govern. After final results, President Isaac Herzog will consult party leaders and ask the person most likely to assemble a coalition to try. That candidate can receive up to six weeks; if the attempt fails, another leader may be given a chance. Arab parties could become decisive even if they do not enter government: outside support or abstention could allow a minority cabinet to pass its investiture vote. Both main blocs have so far rejected partnership with them, which is why an apparent opposition edge is not yet a governing victory. If negotiations fail, another election is possible and Netanyahu's government would remain in caretaker office.

You already know

Exit polls show both main blocs short of 61 and a historically consequential Arab-party delegation; the formal coalition process follows certified results.

New now

Voters appear to have removed the incumbent bloc's majority without giving the opposition a self-sufficient replacement majority.

Exit-poll arithmetic

Opposition 52–53; Netanyahu bloc 50; Arab parties 17–18; governing threshold 61.

Unresolved

Final results may move seats, and no coalition agreement or presidential mandate exists yet.

03 · Qatar, consumer business & capital marketsQatar News Agency / company statement · Tuesday

Dandy's QAR 206 million initial public offering was oversubscribed, giving Qatar's exchange another locally rooted consumer listing.

What changed

The dairy producer completed a 41.2 million-share offer at QAR 5 per share: 28.84 million public shares and 12.36 million institutional shares, with founders retaining 60% and free float expected at 40% after listing.

Why it matters

Oversubscription is evidence of demand at the offer price, while the 40% free float creates enough public ownership for price discovery. It is not evidence that post-listing returns or operating growth are assured.

Watch next

Final allocation and refunds, the confirmed listing date, first-week liquidity, use of proceeds, margins and governance after conversion. The offer result and structure are confirmed; the degree of oversubscription was not disclosed.

The offer widens ownership, but founders still retain a controlling majority

Dandy said demand exceeded the available shares during the 7–20 October subscription period. The public tranche comprised 28.84 million shares, equal to 70% of the offering, while institutions received 12.36 million shares through book-building. At QAR 5 per share, the total offer value is QAR 206 million; public subscribers paid QAR 5.01 including listing and offering fees. After completion, founding shareholders are expected to own 60% of issued capital and the listed free float 40%. That balance matters. A 40% float can support trading and outside scrutiny, while a 60% founder stake preserves control and alignment—but also means minority shareholders cannot direct strategy on their own. QNA's report does not disclose how many times the offer was covered, final allocations, a listing date or how proceeds will translate into capacity and earnings. Oversubscription closes the financing stage; it does not settle valuation.

The offer size, price, tranche split, founder retention and expected free float are disclosed in the company statement carried by QNA.

Curiosity Corner

A turkey-sized 'hell chicken' appears to have mashed soft fruit with a parrot-like forward-and-back chewing stroke 66 million years ago.

The nearly complete skull of Infernogallus jamesi preserves a jaw-muscle arrangement unknown in other non-bird theropods but convergent with fruit-eating parrots. Ridges and grooves on opposing mouth surfaces could process soft food even without teeth. The anatomy makes specialised fruit eating a strong hypothesis, not a fossilised menu: direct gut contents are absent and the paper says its role in spreading flowering plants remains unclear.

The Long View · Daily history

6 min

Britain & the Irish Sea · 1854–1879

LV

The weather forecast became possible when the telegraph made distant observations simultaneous—and a shipwreck made delay intolerable.

Robert FitzRoy did not invent weather. He assembled a system that could observe conditions along a coast, transmit them before the storm arrived, turn them into a judgment and display that judgment where sailors could act. The forecast began as infrastructure: instruments, standards, wires, charts, signals and institutional permission.

Why remember this?

Prediction creates public value only when observation reaches a decision-maker faster than the hazard reaches the public. The first national forecast was therefore not one brilliant calculation but a chain that compressed time. Its early suppression also showed that a service can be scientifically imperfect yet socially valuable enough for users to demand its return.
1 · Before a network, weather knowledge arrived too late to warn anyone

Nineteenth-century sailors understood clouds, wind shifts and falling barometers, but each observation was local. A storm moving across the Bay of Biscay or Irish Sea might be visible in one port hours before it reached another, yet the information could not outrun the weather. The Meteorological Department founded under Robert FitzRoy in 1854 initially focused on marine climatology: collecting standardised observations from ships so recurring wind and pressure patterns could be studied. That produced knowledge about seasons and routes. A warning required something different—many stations reporting at nearly the same moment, a central office able to see the pattern and a channel back to ports before conditions changed. The electric telegraph supplied the missing speed.

2 · The Royal Charter gale converted a scientific possibility into a public demand

On 25 and 26 October 1859, a slow-moving storm crossed the British Isles with winds estimated above 100 miles per hour. The Met Office records 800 deaths, 133 ships lost and another 90 badly damaged. The steam clipper Royal Charter, returning from Melbourne, broke apart on the Anglesey coast; about 459 people died and only 40 survived. FitzRoy reconstructed the storm from coastal observations and argued that its path could have been tracked. The disaster did not make forecasting accurate overnight. It made the cost of failing to organise existing information impossible to ignore. Government allowed the Meteorological Office to test a national storm-warning service despite prominent scientific doubt that weather could be predicted meaningfully.

3 · Telegraph reports turned scattered instruments into one operational picture

From 1 September 1860, coastal stations sent weather observations to London by electric telegraph. FitzRoy could compare pressure, temperature, wind strength and direction across locations on the same day, then draw charts showing how a system was moving. The first storm warning went out on 5 February 1861. Ports displayed combinations of cones and drums on tall staffs, a low-bandwidth code visible from harbour and sea. The signal did not explain atmospheric physics. It translated a central judgment into an immediate decision: stay, shelter or prepare. On the Tyne an early warning was ignored and lives were lost; its accuracy helped persuade other mariners to heed later signals. The service became popular because its output was actionable, not because every mechanism was settled.

4 · FitzRoy slipped a prediction underneath the observations

Daily Weather Reports already circulated to newspapers. On 31 July 1861, FitzRoy added a forecast for the following two days, dividing Britain into broad regions and using plain terms such as fine, moderate and fresh. The Times printed it on 1 August. He had permission for maritime warnings, not a general land forecast, so the public service began partly through administrative improvisation. The language was cautious because the data and models were crude. The east of the country was absent from the first version and added days later. Yet the institutional leap was enormous: an official office was no longer recording what had happened. It was accepting responsibility for a statement about what was likely to happen next.

5 · Error became a political problem because people could now plan around the forecast

A forecast changes behaviour, so false alarms and missed storms carry visible costs. FitzRoy refined his regions and terminology as observations accumulated, but inevitable failures attracted criticism from scientists who doubted both the methods and the office's authority. The pressure affected his health; he died by suicide in 1865. A review then recommended that the office retreat to data collection. Public forecasts ended in May 1866, and storm warnings were also stopped. This looked methodologically cautious, but it removed a service mariners had incorporated into decisions. When deaths increased, public and parliamentary pressure forced storm warnings to resume in 1867. General public forecasts did not return until 1879.

6 · The durable invention was the observation-to-action chain

Modern forecasting uses satellites, radar, numerical models and supercomputers that FitzRoy could not imagine. Its operating logic is still recognisable. Instruments create comparable observations; communications gather them before conditions change; models and forecasters turn them into probabilities; warnings translate probability into action; and feedback reveals where the system failed. The first service was rough and contested, but it proved a network could make the atmosphere legible at a useful speed. The lesson is not that imperfect predictions should be trusted blindly. It is that accuracy, communication and action are separate design problems—and public safety depends on all three working before the hazard arrives.
