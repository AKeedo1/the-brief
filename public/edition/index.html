01 · Saudi Arabia, Yemen & aviationAP / Reuters / flight trackers · Thursday

Riyadh’s airport repeatedly stopped operating after another claimed Houthi attack, cancelling more than 275 flights and pushing major airlines to suspend service.

What changed

Explosions were reported in Riyadh for a second day and King Khalid International Airport stopped arrivals and departures for extended periods. FlightAware counted more than 275 cancellations on Thursday—about 40 percent of arrivals and 30 percent of departures. Lufthansa Group suspended Riyadh flights through 16 October; Air India, Akasa Air and IndiGo announced shorter suspensions. Reuters reported that a parked Saudia aircraft was badly damaged by fire, according to three people briefed on the matter, but Saudi authorities had not confirmed the cause or casualties.

Why it matters

The security story has become an operating interruption at one of the Gulf’s major hubs. Repeated closures affect network reliability, aircraft positioning, crews, connections, insurance and travellers even when runways remain structurally usable. They also test whether new regional defence assistance can reduce risk quickly enough to restore carrier confidence rather than merely add equipment.

Watch next

Saudi confirmation of the latest damage; airport and airspace notices; further airline suspensions; Qatar Airways and other Gulf-carrier scheduling; the number and type of weapons launched; interception performance; insurance guidance; and whether normal operations hold for several days rather than reopen between alerts.

Airline confidence depends on predictability, not one successful reopening

An airport can clear debris and resume movements while remaining unreliable as a network node. Airlines must position aircraft and crews, preserve legal duty time, protect connecting itineraries and know that a diversion airport will remain available. A closure of an hour can cascade through a long-haul schedule; repeated closures make the probability of recurrence more important than the damage from any one strike. Carriers therefore respond to notices, intelligence, insurer advice and their own risk thresholds, not only to an official declaration that the runway is open.

The winter campaign is attacking movement, electricity and the ability to recover.

02 · Ukraine & civilian infrastructureUkrainian authorities / AP · Thursday

A Russian guided bomb exploded beside public buses in Kramatorsk, killing 30 people as emergency blackouts spread in Kyiv after the previous day’s mass barrage.

What changed

Ukrainian authorities said the bomb struck next to a bus stop in the eastern city of Kramatorsk, leading local officials to suspend public transport. Follow-on small-drone attacks reportedly impeded rescuers. The strike came one day after a missile killed 22 people, including five children, in Pryluky. Grid operator Ukrenergo ordered emergency outages in Kyiv as recent attacks continued against energy, rail, telecoms and other civilian systems.

Why it matters

The sequence shows a campaign aimed at both human movement and the systems that keep cities functioning before winter. A bus stop is not strategic infrastructure in the conventional sense, but public transport is part of daily economic capacity. Repeated attacks also force air defences, repair crews and emergency services to divide scarce resources across immediate rescue and long-duration grid resilience.

Watch next

Independent confirmation of the weapon and target; revised casualty figures; whether follow-on drones continue targeting responders; the duration of emergency outages; attacks on generation and transmission assets; the Friday U.S. meetings on air defence; and concrete deliveries of interceptors rather than political statements.

Urban resilience is a chain, not a single target

Power, transport, communications and emergency response reinforce one another. Electricity runs signalling, pumping and telecoms; public transport moves workers and residents when fuel is scarce; communications coordinate repair. Repeated aerial attacks can produce larger effects by breaking several links at once or by forcing cities to protect everything with too few interceptors. Follow-on strikes create a further dilemma: rescue and repair must begin quickly, but teams operating in the open become exposed to a second attack.

The global economy

Trade is growing faster, but almost half the first-half gain came from one investment cycle.

The forecast is an official estimate, not observed full-year growth; services and regional outcomes remain much weaker than the goods headline.
03 · Global trade, AI & travelWorld Trade Organization / AP / Reuters · Thursday

The WTO doubled its 2026 goods-trade growth forecast to 3.9 percent, but the upgrade is unusually concentrated in AI hardware while transport and travel weakened.

What changed

The World Trade Organization raised its 2026 merchandise-trade forecast from 1.9 percent to 3.9 percent and now expects 4.1 percent growth in 2027. AI-enabling goods such as semiconductors and servers accounted for 47 percent of global merchandise-trade growth in the first half, with trade in those products up 67 percent year on year. The WTO simultaneously cut its 2026 services-trade forecast from 4.8 percent to 3.3 percent as Middle East disruption raised transport costs and reduced travel.

Why it matters

The headline says resilience, but the composition says concentration. Physical AI investment is strong enough to offset large regional losses in energy and shipping, while tourism and aviation remain exposed. That makes the trade outlook sensitive to two narrow engines: continued data-centre capital spending and the ability of supply chains to reroute around conflict without exhausting cost and inventory buffers.

Watch next

Semiconductor and server orders; hyperscaler capital spending; Middle East shipping and flight volumes; freight and insurance costs; tourist arrivals; whether non-AI manufacturing joins the expansion; and the WTO’s 2027 assumptions if energy disruption persists.

Goods can reroute more easily than a trip or a flight

A cargo buyer can sometimes switch suppliers, ports or shipping corridors and accept a higher cost. A traveller may cancel entirely when routes, prices and perceived safety deteriorate. The WTO found that alternative energy and fertilizer suppliers limited global volume losses even as Middle Eastern exports fell sharply. AI infrastructure added a second buffer by creating exceptional demand for chips, servers and related equipment. Services do not receive the same inventory benefit: an empty hotel night or cancelled flight cannot be stored and sold later.

Physical constraints

The question has shifted from barrels paused offshore to infrastructure exposed onshore.

Intensity and the forecast track are measured; exact landfall damage and outage duration remain unknown.
04 · Weather, energy & logisticsNational Hurricane Center / AP · Thursday night

Hurricane Isaias strengthened to Category 2 with 100-mile-per-hour winds and was expected to remain dangerous through Friday’s U.S. Gulf Coast landfall.

What changed

National Hurricane Center aircraft and satellite data supported raising Isaias to 100 miles per hour on Thursday afternoon. The storm turned northeast toward the northern Gulf Coast, with surge, wind and heavy-rain warnings in effect. The previous edition recorded roughly 511,000 barrels a day of offshore oil production already shut; the new information is the storm’s rapid strengthening and clearer expectation that it will remain dangerous through landfall.

Why it matters

The operating risk is moving from precautionary offshore shutdowns to the onshore system. Refineries, ports, roads and electricity can take longer to restore than platforms, and Friday timing leaves little margin for further preparation. A stronger storm also raises the chance that energy disruption persists after crude production can technically restart.

Watch next

The final track and intensity; surge height; landfall location; refinery and port status; grid outages; offshore inspection and restart timing; inland rainfall; airport closures; and whether fuel-product prices react differently from crude because conversion capacity is hit.

A storm’s category does not fully describe its economic footprint

Wind category measures maximum sustained wind near the centre. Economic damage also depends on storm size, surge, rainfall, forward speed, landfall angle and what lies in the path. Ports and refineries combine water, electricity, specialised equipment and tightly sequenced safety procedures; each can delay the others. That is why a short upstream shutdown can remain manageable while a damaged refinery, terminal or grid creates a longer product shortage.

05 · Qatar, energy & capital allocationQatarEnergy / QNA · Thursday

QatarEnergy won 30 percent interests in seven ultra-deep-water exploration blocks off Brazil, extending its upstream option set with Petrobras rather than adding near-term production.

What changed

A QatarEnergy–Petrobras consortium won seven blocks in Brazil’s sixth concession open-acreage round. Petrobras will operate with 70 percent and QatarEnergy will hold 30 percent. The acreage covers about 4,700 square kilometres in the Ceará Basin along Brazil’s northeastern Equatorial Margin, at water depths of roughly 2,000 to 3,600 metres. Concession contracts are expected in the first half of 2027.

Why it matters

This is exploration exposure, not booked output. It gives QatarEnergy another route to future offshore resources while sharing technical and financial risk with Brazil’s national operator. Ultra-deep water and the Equatorial Margin can offer large discoveries, but they also require expensive appraisal, long development cycles and environmental approvals before any commercial value is known.

Watch next

Contract signature; work commitments and spending; environmental licensing; seismic and drilling schedules; the consortium’s first prospect; recoverable-resource estimates; oil-price assumptions; and whether QatarEnergy adds or reduces interests before development decisions.

Exploration acreage is a staged option

Winning a block usually commits partners to studies, seismic work and drilling over time. Each stage buys better information before the largest capital decision. If geology disappoints, partners can stop after spending the agreed work programme; if it succeeds, appraisal, engineering, approval and construction still follow. The operator matters because deep-water execution depends on subsurface knowledge, drilling capability and local regulatory experience. Petrobras provides that operating base while QatarEnergy gains participation without leading the project.

Local markets

A sound earnings print could not outrun the regional risk premium.

The reported profit and market close are facts; the relative weight of geopolitics, positioning and company-specific selling is inferred.
06 · Qatar banking & marketsQNB / QSE / QNA / Reuters · Thursday

QNB’s nine-month profit rose 4 percent to QAR 13.3 billion, but Qatar’s equity index fell to its lowest close since June 2020 as regional risk overwhelmed earnings relief.

What changed

QNB reported net profit of QAR 13.3 billion for the first nine months, up from QAR 12.8 billion a year earlier; earnings per share rose to QAR 1.37 and assets increased 5 percent to QAR 1.46 trillion. On the same day, the Qatar Stock Exchange index fell 2.21 percent to 9,005.61. Reuters reported that the benchmark reached its lowest level since the pandemic selloff of June 2020, with QNB shares down 2.9 percent.

Why it matters

The divergence separates company performance from the price investors demand for regional exposure. QNB’s growth and asset base show operating resilience, but equity prices can fall when war, shipping risk, oil volatility and risk aversion raise the required return across the market. One session does not invalidate the earnings; it shows that macro risk is setting the valuation frame.

Watch next

QNB’s detailed margins, asset quality and international contribution; deposit and loan growth; further results across Qatar; foreign flows; the index’s ability to hold 9,000; shipping incidents near Qatar; and whether regional risk premiums ease rather than merely reverse for a day.

Earnings and valuation answer different questions

Profit describes what the bank earned during a period. The share price discounts future cash flows using assumptions about growth, credit losses, funding and the return investors require. A regional shock can lift that required return immediately even when the latest accounts remain healthy. Large index weights amplify the effect: selling in a major bank can pull the whole benchmark lower, while a falling benchmark can trigger further passive or risk-limit selling.

Technology and capital

The AI boom is visible in shipped wafers, while the return on all that infrastructure remains a separate test.

Revenue is reported; the mix, margin, durability and end-customer economics await full results and later evidence.
07 · Semiconductors & investingTSMC / SEC / Reuters · Thursday

TSMC’s third-quarter revenue reached a record NT$1.49 trillion, about 50 percent above a year earlier and ahead of expectations as AI demand reached the foundry floor.

What changed

TSMC reported September revenue of NT$511.86 billion, up 54.6 percent from a year earlier and only 0.6 percent below August’s record. The three-month total was roughly NT$1.49 trillion, or US$46.7 billion, above the NT$1.46 trillion analyst estimate cited by Reuters and above the top end of the company’s prior dollar guidance. Revenue for the first nine months rose 41.1 percent year on year.

Why it matters

Unlike valuation or spending commitments, foundry revenue records chips that customers have ordered and TSMC has manufactured. It is therefore one of the cleanest physical indicators that the AI infrastructure boom remains real. It does not by itself prove end-user returns or sustainable margins; orders can still reflect inventory building, constrained capacity or customers racing to secure supply.

A foundry is downstream of promises and upstream of usable products

Cloud companies can announce capital budgets before equipment is installed, and chip designers can announce road maps before volume production. TSMC sits between those intentions and finished systems. Revenue means manufacturing capacity was used and wafers were sold, making it harder than a narrative headline—but still not final demand. Chips can accumulate in inventory, and extraordinary scarcity can pull orders forward. The strongest confirmation would combine revenue, utilisation, margins and sustained customer deployment.

Technology policy

Talent access has become another contested technology supply chain.

The suspensions were announced; the government’s allegations are disputed and their legal basis, scope and duration need documentation.
08 · Technology labour & immigrationWhite House statements / Microsoft / AP · Thursday

The U.S. administration suspended Microsoft and seven other technology firms from an employment-based green-card programme, alleging abuse that the companies have not yet had tested in court.

What changed

Vice President JD Vance said Microsoft, Adobe, Cognizant, Infosys, Tata, Wipro, HCL and Capgemini would be suspended from a programme used to sponsor foreign workers for permanent residence. He alleged Microsoft paired U.S. layoffs with foreign-worker hiring. Microsoft said 80 percent of roughly 6,000 H-1B filings last fiscal year extended or changed status for existing employees and that new hires represented about 1 percent of its U.S. workforce. The administration also announced scrutiny of university exchange programmes.

Why it matters

The action shifts technology immigration policy from higher fees and rhetoric to firm-specific exclusion. Green cards reduce a worker’s dependence on one employer, so blocking sponsorship may protect some domestic recruitment channels while also making skilled foreign employees less mobile. For technology companies, talent policy is becoming an operational and legal risk alongside export controls and procurement rules.

Watch next

The legal instrument and duration of suspension; evidence supporting the fraud allegations; company challenges; Department of Labor procedure; whether current employees lose active cases; wider enforcement against H-1B and J-1 users; and changes to hiring locations or research investment.

A temporary work visa and permanent residence solve different dependency problems

An H-1B visa generally ties a worker’s ability to remain in the country to qualifying employment. A green card gives permanent residence and makes changing employers easier. The programme at issue concerns the route from temporary status to permanence, including a labour-market test. That distinction matters because suspending sponsorship can reduce an employer’s recruiting tool while also keeping existing foreign workers more dependent on temporary status. The policy effect therefore cannot be inferred from the political label alone.

Public legitimacy

AI’s capability curve is outrunning the political coalition that must absorb its consequences.

The survey measures U.S. attitudes at one point in time; it does not establish global opinion or predict behaviour.
09 · AI, public opinion & regulationAP–NORC probability survey · Thursday

Nearly two thirds of U.S. adults say AI is developing too fast, while roughly eight in ten want government to prioritise worker protection and human control.

What changed

An AP–NORC survey of 2,140 U.S. adults found 64 percent believe AI is developing too quickly, 27 percent say the pace is about right and 8 percent say it is too slow. Large majorities across parties rated keeping AI under human control and protecting workers as highly important government priorities. Only about half gave the same weight to promoting innovation, and fewer prioritised competition with China. The poll ran 24–28 September with a margin of sampling error of plus or minus 2.9 percentage points.

Why it matters

Public legitimacy is becoming a constraint on deployment, not just model capability. Voters appear more aligned on desired outcomes than on a specific regulatory instrument: control and worker protection are popular, but those preferences do not specify testing standards, liability, labour policy or acceptable delay. Politicians can therefore claim the same mandate while proposing very different rules.

Watch next

Whether concern changes voting or only survey responses; support for specific mandatory rules; data-centre and labour disputes in local races; trust after new incidents; partisan movement; and whether adoption continues rising even as stated concern remains high.

Broad concern does not automatically create a workable rule

People can agree that systems should remain under human control while disagreeing about what control means in medicine, transport, defence or office software. They can support worker protection while preferring retraining, wage insurance, disclosure, bargaining rights or limits on automation. Polling is most useful here as a boundary condition: a policy that offers only faster innovation is misaligned with the public priorities measured. The instrument still requires technical design and evidence.

The probability-based survey found cross-party concern about speed, control and employment effects.

New now

The scale of concern is now measured nationally as policymakers debate voluntary versus mandatory safeguards.

Measured concern

Sixty-four percent said development is too fast; only 8 percent said it is too slow.

The Long View · Daily history

7 min

TAIWAN
1985–PRESENT

042

TSMC’s founding move was not a better chip. It was a promise not to compete with the companies whose chips it manufactured.

In 1987, Morris Chang and Taiwan built the first dedicated semiconductor foundry. By separating design from fabrication, they lowered the cost of starting a chip company and created a scale machine whose neutrality became as important as its engineering.

Why remember this?

A platform can become indispensable by refusing the most tempting adjacent business. TSMC’s discipline—manufacture for customers, never sell competing chips—turned trust into volume, volume into process learning and process learning into an industrial lead that reshaped the geography of technology.
1 · The old model bundled invention and factories

For much of semiconductor history, the leading companies were integrated device manufacturers. They designed chips, fabricated them in their own plants and sold the finished products. Some factories accepted outside work when they had spare capacity, but a customer was entrusting its design to a company that might also compete with it. The capital barrier was equally severe. A new design business needed access to expensive fabrication before it could prove demand, and each generation of process technology made the factory larger, cleaner and more costly. Many good ideas therefore arrived attached to a financing problem and a conflict-of-interest problem.

2 · Taiwan had manufacturing ambition but no dominant chip franchise

Morris Chang had spent decades in the U.S. semiconductor industry before returning to Taiwan to lead the Industrial Technology Research Institute. In his Computer History Museum oral history, he described Taiwan’s likely strength as manufacturing rather than a ready-made market, global brand or large community of chip designers. A conventional integrated company would have forced Taiwan to compete simultaneously in product selection, design, marketing and fabrication. A pure-play foundry narrowed the problem: become exceptionally good at making other companies’ designs. TSMC was founded in February 1987 by Taiwan’s government, Philips and private investors around that choice.

3 · The product was trustworthy capacity

TSMC’s central promise was structural. It would manufacture customers’ chips but would not design and sell its own competing product line. That removed a fear that an overflow foundry could learn from a customer and later attack its market. The promise also changed incentives inside the factory. Process development, yield, delivery and customer service were not support functions for an internal chip division; they were the whole business. TSMC called the model a dedicated or pure-play foundry. What it sold was more than wafer output. It sold credible neutrality, repeatable manufacturing and the confidence that a customer’s success would expand rather than threaten the supplier.

4 · Neutrality unlocked the fabless company

Once entrepreneurs could rent advanced manufacturing, they no longer needed to finance a leading-edge factory before testing a chip design. Design and fabrication became separate businesses connected by technical standards, design tools and long operating relationships. That division of labour widened the field of companies able to create processors, communications chips and specialised accelerators. TSMC’s own explanation says the model lowered the threshold for establishing an integrated-circuit design company. The result was not outsourcing in the simple sense of moving an existing task. It created a new class of firm whose business model would have been difficult to sustain without a neutral manufacturer.

5 · Many customers created a manufacturing flywheel

A foundry serving many designers can pool demand across products and markets. Greater utilisation helps absorb the fixed cost of a fab; greater volume produces more data about defects and yield; better yield lowers unit cost and strengthens delivery; those advantages attract more customers and finance the next process generation. The loop is difficult to start because early customers fear delay and poor yield, and it is difficult to catch once scale compounds. TSMC’s 1996 annual report recorded that it became profitable in December 1988, less than two years after founding, then expanded through successive fabs and international customer offices.

6 · Public capital accelerated the scale race

TSMC listed its shares in Taiwan in September 1994 and its American depositary shares in New York on 8 October 1997. The New York offering arrived as the company planned billions of dollars of fab investment. Public markets did not invent the model, but they helped finance a business in which staying competitive required repeated, enormous capital commitments before the next generation’s revenue was certain. By 2025, TSMC said it manufactured 12,682 products across 305 technologies for 534 customers. The original narrow promise had become a diversified production network without turning TSMC into a merchant chip designer.

7 · Success concentrated a global dependency

The same separation that multiplied chip design also concentrated advanced fabrication in a small number of firms and locations. Customers gained freedom from owning fabs but became dependent on foundry capacity, packaging, equipment and geopolitical stability. Governments now subsidise new plants and treat semiconductor supply as national security. TSMC is building outside Taiwan, yet a factory is not portable merely because money is available: yields depend on equipment, suppliers, utilities, engineers and accumulated operating knowledge. The modern dilemma is therefore produced by the model’s success. Neutral scale made innovation cheaper and faster, then made the neutral manufacturer a chokepoint the world cannot easily duplicate.

Carriers therefore respond to notices, intelligence, insurer advice and their own risk thresholds, not only to an official declaration that the runway is open.

The update

Riyadh’s airport repeatedly stopped operating after another claimed Houthi attack, cancelling more than 275 flights and pushing major airlines to suspend service.

Explosions were reported in Riyadh for a second day and King Khalid International Airport stopped arrivals and departures for extended periods. FlightAware counted more than 275 cancellations on Thursday—about 40 percent of arrivals and 30 percent of departures. Lufthansa Group suspended Riyadh flights through 16 October; Air India, Akasa Air and IndiGo announced shorter suspensions. Reuters reported that a parked Saudia aircraft was badly damaged by fire, according to three people briefed on the matter, but Saudi authorities had not confirmed the cause or casualties.

Why the interpretation changed

The security story has become an operating interruption at one of the Gulf’s major hubs. Repeated closures affect network reliability, aircraft positioning, crews, connections, insurance and travellers even when runways remain structurally usable. They also test whether new regional defence assistance can reduce risk quickly enough to restore carrier confidence rather than merely add equipment.
