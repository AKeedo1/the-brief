01 · Qatar, mobility & public safetyOfficial Gazette and Ministry of Interior · Wednesday

Qatar’s new Traffic Law starts on 19 December, with minimum highway speeds and stronger distracted-driving enforcement.

What changed

Law No. 19 of 2026 was published on 18 November and will take effect after 30 days; the Ministry of Interior says vehicles may be impounded for up to 90 days for mobile-phone use and other distractions, and a first phase of minimum and maximum highway speeds is coming.

Why it matters

This is an actionable change to everyday life and business fleets: the law moves beyond fines toward vehicle availability, creates a specific offence for unjustifiably slow driving and gives owners six months to bring registered vehicles into compliance.

Watch next

The executive regulation, which roads receive minimum speeds first, how camera evidence and appeals work, the exact impoundment process, and the compliance guidance for company-owned and delivery vehicles.

The law adds new behaviour to the enforcement system rather than only increasing familiar penalties

The replacement Traffic Law contains 137 articles and supersedes the 2007 law. It explicitly accommodates newer modes of transport, permits minimum as well as maximum speed limits and treats unjustifiably slow driving as a safety problem when it obstructs traffic. The Ministry of Interior says distracted driving, including handheld mobile-phone use, can expose a vehicle to impoundment for as long as 90 days. The law also preserves a points system in which repeated offences lower the threshold for licence suspension, and it gives owners of already registered vehicles up to six months to adjust their status. The practical impact will depend on the executive regulation, signs, enforcement technology and whether fleet owners can identify and correct repeat risk before a vehicle disappears from service.

You already know

The law was published on 18 November, takes effect on 19 December and authorises stronger measures against distracted and obstructive driving.

New now

Minimum highway speeds and extended vehicle impoundment make the vehicle itself—not only the driver’s wallet—part of the deterrent.

Driver consequence

A distracted-driving offence may remove the vehicle from use for up to 90 days.

System consequence

Minimum speeds require visible limits, consistent detection and safe exceptions for congestion, weather and vehicle condition.

Economy & markets

The argument has moved from whether tightening resumes to how quickly it proceeds.

Minutes describe the October discussion; they are not a December decision.
02 · Economy, rates & investingFederal Reserve · Wednesday

The Federal Reserve’s October minutes make another rate increase the baseline, not a tail risk.

What changed

Almost all meeting participants judged that further increases would probably be needed, a couple said more than 25 basis points could merit consideration in December if inflation data worsen, and two voters had already preferred a quarter-point rise in October.

Why it matters

The direction raises the discount rate applied to property, equities and business investment while borrowing costs remain high; the minutes also show inflation broadening beyond energy and tariffs into technology goods and services linked to the AI buildout.

Watch next

The 8–9 December meeting, November inflation and labour data, whether the committee chooses 25 or 50 basis points, and whether higher long yields tighten conditions before the Fed acts.

The committee sees a resilient economy and a more persistent inflation problem

The Federal Open Market Committee held its target range at 3.75–4.00% in October by a 10–2 vote. Staff estimated September PCE inflation at 3.6% and core PCE at 3.1%, both higher than a year earlier. Participants said energy remained a major pressure, but many also saw demand from data-centre construction straining supplies and raising prices for technology-related goods. Almost all expected more tightening under their baseline outlook. The complication is that the same AI investment supporting growth is also adding inflation and financial-stability risk: rapid debt growth, leases and opaque financing could amplify losses if returns disappoint. Higher rates therefore cool demand while also increasing the financing burden on the investment cycle the Fed is watching.

You already know

The October rate was unchanged, two voters dissented for an increase and almost all participants expected further tightening.

New now

A couple of participants put a larger-than-quarter-point December move into the discussion if incoming inflation is unfavourable.

Inflation case

Staff estimated total PCE inflation at 3.6% and core at 3.1% in September, above the 2% objective.

Financing case

Higher rates also pressure leveraged AI projects, property and smaller businesses before inflation is visibly back at target.

Aviation & tourism

More passengers solved the load-factor problem but not the profit problem.

Jet2 remains profitable and cash-rich; this is margin pressure, not a demand collapse.
03 · Aviation, tourism & consumer demandJet2 · Wednesday

Jet2 carried a record 15.4 million passengers—but lower pricing and higher costs cut operating profit by 18%.

What changed

First-half revenue rose 5% to a record £5.63 billion and passengers rose 9%, while operating profit fell to £588.3 million; Jet2 has removed 240,000 winter seats since September and withheld full-year guidance.

Why it matters

The result separates traffic from economics: price investment can fill aircraft and grow revenue while weakening margin, a useful signal for airlines and destinations measuring success through passenger counts alone.

Watch next

Winter booking pace, unhedged jet-fuel prices, the 14% decline in flight-only yield, performance of the new Gatwick base and whether reduced capacity restores pricing without losing customers.

Record demand was bought partly through price, then fuel forced a winter correction

Jet2 increased first-half passengers by 9% to 15.4 million and lifted load factor 1.5 percentage points to 89.7%. The mix shifted sharply toward flight-only travel: those passengers rose 20%, while package-holiday customers increased 4%. Flight-only yield per passenger fell 14% as the company used lower prices to fill capacity amid geopolitical uncertainty and late booking. Revenue still reached a record £5.63 billion, but operating profit fell 18% and pre-tax profit fell 19%. For winter, the group has removed 240,000 seats since early September because of elevated fuel prices on its unhedged requirement. The operating question is whether capacity discipline can recover unit economics without weakening the integrated holiday model that differentiates Jet2 from a pure airline.

You already know

Passenger volume, revenue and load factor reached records while profit and unit yield declined.

New now

Management is actively cutting winter capacity and says fuel volatility and incomplete bookings make full-year guidance premature.

Volume

15.4 million passengers, 89.7% load factor and £5.63 billion of revenue.

Economics

£588.3 million operating profit, down 18%, with flight-only yield per passenger down 14%.

Curiosity Corner

A Milky Way simulation processed as many as 5.4 trillion particles—but the breakthrough is the method, not a finished digital galaxy.

Researchers ran the same architecture-portable code across three exascale GPU systems. On Aurora, 60,236 GPUs processed up to 144 billion particles each second; the largest test used 5.4 trillion particles, roughly four orders of magnitude beyond conventional galaxy simulations. An AI surrogate handles the short, expensive phase after a supernova so the physics code can advance without every region waiting on the smallest time step. The team is explicit that this is still a performance demonstration. The next step is a scientifically useful, star-by-star Milky Way run. That distinction is the interesting part: computing scale is not the discovery, but it can make a previously unaskable scientific question testable.

The Long View · Daily history

5 min

1993–2019

07

The Federal Reserve did not always announce interest-rate decisions—markets once had to infer policy from its trading desk

Immediate statements, explicit rate targets, faster minutes and press conferences now feel inevitable. They were assembled over 25 years after the old system gave sophisticated traders an advantage and turned central-bank silence into a market signal.

Why remember this?

1 · Before 1994, a monetary-policy decision could be public in effect but private in form

The Federal Open Market Committee changed the supply and price of reserves through the New York Fed’s trading desk, but it did not routinely announce the result when a meeting ended. Market participants watched the desk’s operations and tried to infer whether the policy stance had changed. Minutes arrived many weeks later. The system was not completely secret—the central bank’s actions moved money markets—but it distributed understanding unevenly. Institutions with people, data and expertise close to the market could decode a signal sooner than the public whose borrowing costs were being changed. Opacity had become part of the operating mechanism.

2 · Pressure for records changed the institution before statements changed the market

By 1993 the Fed began publishing FOMC minutes in the form familiar today. The following year it began releasing meeting transcripts with a five-year lag. These steps reflected a larger argument about accountability: an independent central bank could protect its freedom to act by explaining the basis of that action, not by withholding it. Transparency also changed internal behaviour. Once records and wording would be published, the committee had to treat communication as part of policy rather than an administrative account written after the important work was finished.

3 · The first immediate statement arrived with a surprise rate rise

On 4 February 1994, the FOMC raised its federal-funds target by 25 basis points to 3.25%, its first increase since 1989. Chairman Alan Greenspan issued a short post-meeting statement describing the action. The decision and the new communication practice were not fully anticipated; interest rates rose sharply and volatility increased. Yet the experiment established a crucial fact: a central bank could state what it had done directly instead of asking markets to reverse-engineer the trading desk. The first statement belonged to the chairman and did not promise that future meetings would be handled the same way.

4 · A one-off announcement became a repeatable public protocol

In February 1995 the committee said every change in policy would be communicated immediately. In July it began stating the intended federal-funds target explicitly rather than relying on coded language about reserve pressure. In 1997 the statement became the committee’s rather than the chairman’s. From January 2000, every scheduled meeting produced a statement whether policy changed or not. Each step removed a different ambiguity: whether there was news, who owned it, what number had changed and whether silence itself carried information.

5 · Communication became an instrument that could move conditions before the rate moved

The Fed shortened the release lag for minutes to about three weeks in 2005, began post-meeting press conferences in 2011, adopted an explicit 2% inflation objective in 2012 and eventually held a press conference after every scheduled meeting in 2019. Greater transparency did not make policy simple. Markets began comparing phrases word by word, forecasts and dissent became tradable information, and guidance about the future could change financial conditions immediately. But the public protocol made the institution more legible and accountable. Today’s minutes matter because a long series of procedural inventions turned the committee’s internal disagreement into a shared economic signal.
